01Audit
What exists

The current site, honestly assessed

ynwas.com is a GoDaddy Website Builder single-pager. Its problem is not that it looks dated — it is that it miscategorises the business, and then undermines itself with unfinished template furniture. There is real material buried in it, though, and it is worth naming precisely before we throw anything away.

Assets — real, verified, reusable

  • KeepThe service inventoryAround twenty concrete line items across three groups: company formation types, document attestation, and commercial/licensing work. This is the single most valuable thing on the site and the backbone of the new IA.
  • KeepA real Doha addressZone 39, Street 840, Building 78 — HUB Business Center, Doha. A physical office is a trust signal most competitors bury; we surface it.
  • KeepDirect contact routes+974 4144 8471, +974 5599 1069, info@ynwas.com, abashar@ynwas.com, 09:00–17:00, Instagram @yanwaservices. A named person in the email address is an asset, not an accident — §18 asks us to make it obvious a real professional is available.
  • KeepThe mission sentence“…so they can stop sacrificing their life for the sake of being a business owner.” Clumsy as written, but it is the actual value proposition and it survives a rewrite.
  • ReworkThe “Why Qatar” copyBroadly accurate and correctly cites Qatar National Vision 2030, but reads as generic. Becomes the seed of the Qatar context page, rewritten against primary sources.

Liabilities — remove or replace

  • Critical“Expert Legal Services for Business Incorporation”The hero sells a law firm. The site elsewhere describes the team as “Public Relations Officers and legal experts.” PRO and corporate-services work is not legal practice, and positioning it as such is both the wrong category commercially and a claim that needs checking before it goes back up. See question 02.
  • Critical“Get 10% off your first purchase”E-commerce boilerplate on a corporate-services site. It tells a serious client that nobody edited this page. Delete outright.
  • CriticalNo Arabic, anywhereA Doha firm serving Gulf business owners with an English-only site. This is the largest single gap and the reason i18n is architectural rather than bolted on.
  • RemoveEmpty template furniture“Our Happy Clients!” with no clients. “My Blog” with no posts and the builder's own default label. Sign In / Create Account / Bookings — a dormant account system with nothing behind it. Each one is a dead end that costs credibility.
  • FixUnfinished copyHero paragraph repeated three times. “etc…” left in the attestation list. A licensing item truncated mid-sentence at “Changing Trade Name / Appointment of…”. Grammar breaks in the FAQ. Footer still reads © 2025 and “Powered by GoDaddy Website Builder”.
  • FixA contact form that learns nothingName and email only. No phone, no country, no service interest, no stage. §7 of the brief wants this site to become a lead-qualification system; the current form is the opposite of one.
  • DecideInconsistent naming“Ynwa”, “YNWA”, “Ynwas”, and @yanwaservices all appear, against the domain ynwas.com. One canonical wordmark has to be chosen before any type is set. See question 01.

What the competition does

The Qatar set-up market divides into two tiers. The bottom tier looks like ynwas.com does now. The top tier — ProPartner Group, Sovereign, CSP — wins on specificity: named consultants with photos and direct lines, concrete timelines, published fee ranges, ISO badges, forty-plus client logos, downloadable guides. None of them wins on design.

That is the opening. YNWA cannot out-claim firms with a decade of logos to show, and per §16 it must not try. It can out-explain them. A site that answers “what do I actually have to do to open a company here” better than anyone else in the market converts on competence rather than on credentials — and it is the one axis where craft, which we control, is the differentiator.

02Positioning
Message

From “legal services” to a company's whole life in Qatar

The category shift is the strategic move. Not a law firm. Not another “business setup agency”. The administrative operator behind a company's whole life in Qatar — from the first licence to every renewal after it.

The journey, mapped to services that already exist

Start → Operate → Grow is not a slogan here. Every service currently listed on ynwas.com falls into one of the three stages without anything being invented:

Why this matters commercially

Formation is a one-time transaction. Operate and Grow are recurring revenue. Structuring the site around the journey rather than around “company formation” reframes YNWA from a vendor you use once into a relationship you keep — and it gives an existing company, who is the easier sale, an obvious door to walk through.

Language to avoid

Per §18 — banned from all copy unless a specific fact sits directly behind it: your trusted partner, seamless solutions, end-to-end services, expert team, world-class, one-stop shop, hassle-free, we've got you covered. 04Content
Model

Content model, with a safety catch built in

§16 forbids inventing anything and §12 demands content stay separate from presentation. Both are satisfied by the same structure: typed content modules where every record carries a verification status, and the build refuses to ship unverified records to production.

Core types

type Locale = 'en' | 'ar'
type L<T> = Record<Locale, T>              // every string is a pair

type Status =
  | 'confirmed'      // client verified — safe to publish
  | 'derived'        // taken verbatim from the old site
  | 'placeholder'    // written by us, must be replaced
  | 'blocked'        // needs a fact we do not have

interface ServiceCategory {
  id: 'company-formation' | 'pro-services' | 'corporate-services'
  stage: 'start' | 'operate' | 'grow'
  slug: L<string>
  title: L<string>
  lede: L<string>
  audience: L<string>                   // who this page is for
  services: Service[]
  process?: ProcessStep[]
  faqIds: string[]
  seo: SeoMeta
  status: Status
}

interface Service {
  id: string
  slug: L<string>
  title: L<string>
  summary: L<string>
  detail?: L<string>                    // unlocks the detail page
  requirements?: L<string[]>            // documents needed
  typicalDuration?: L<string>           // blocked until confirmed
  relatedIds: string[]
  status: Status
}

interface Faq {
  id: string
  question: L<string>
  answer: L<string>
  categoryId?: string
  sources?: { label: string; url: string }[]
  schemaEligible: boolean               // only true when verified
  status: Status
}

interface Testimonial {
  id: string; quote: L<string>; author: string
  role: L<string>; company: string; logo?: ImageRef
  status: Status                        // none exist yet — array is empty
}
The safety catch

A single helper, publishable(record), gates every render. In development, anything not confirmed renders with a dotted outline and a corner tag naming its status. In a production build, a script walks the content tree and fails the build if a blocked record is reachable from a live route.

The effect: a fabricated testimonial cannot reach production by accident, an empty proof section hides itself instead of rendering “Our Happy Clients!” over nothing, and the client's outstanding items are visible in the UI rather than buried in a checklist.

Typed modules rather than a CMS for v1: they are type-safe, reviewable in a diff, and cost nothing to host. The shapes above map cleanly onto Sanity or Payload documents when the client wants to edit content themselves — that is a migration, not a rewrite.

05Visual
Direction

Modern Institutional

The reference is not a competitor website. It is the printed material of a serious professional practice: limestone, ink, a single ceremonial accent, and a great deal of restraint. This document is set in the system it proposes.

Palette

#8A1538 is Qatar's flag maroon. It is the one deliberate national reference in the system and it earns its place — but it is a scalpel, not a wash. It appears on primary buttons, active states, section numbers and nothing else. Flooded across large surfaces it reads as a government portal, which §4 explicitly forbids. Brass carries the small metallic details a maroon-heavy palette would otherwise have to; the neutrals are warmed toward the accent rather than left as unconsidered grey.

Dark theme lifts the accent to #CF4B6C — #8A1538 fails contrast on a dark ground — and drops the paper to a cool #101114 so the warm neutrals stay legible as warm.

Arabic is not the Latin scale reused. Arabic sets roughly 8% larger with ~15% more leading at the same optical weight — the script has no ascender/descender rhythm to carry tight line-height, and cramped Arabic is the single clearest sign a site was designed in English first. Two locale-aware scales, one set of components.

Upgrade path if the client will license: 29LT Bukra or TheSans Arabic for display would lift the Arabic further. Noto Naskh is the strong free option and ships v1 without compromise.

The Qatari reference beyond the maroon is structural, not decorative: the mashrabiya screen as a fine 1px lattice used at very low opacity behind the hero and the closing CTA, and nowhere else. Set in CSS, not shipped as an image. No sand dunes, no skyline stock, no falcon iconography.

Imagery direction: architectural detail and real workplace photography over stock handshakes. Until real photography exists, sections are composed to work with type and rule alone — the layout must not depend on images we do not have.

09Open
Questions

What we need from the client

Nothing here blocks phases A–C. All of it blocks launch. The first two are now settled; the remaining seven are still open with the client.

Identity — resolved 02 Sep
  1. Canonical name and wordmark. ResolvedNo logo existed, so one was drawn. The wordmark is YNWA; ynwas.com remains the domain. Construction, scale, lockups and usage are in the identity document. Still worth asking the client whether YNWA is an acronym that should be expanded anywhere.
  2. The word “legal”. ResolvedThe work is government and administrative process, not legal practice. No page describes it as legal services, and the footer carries an explicit non-advice line in both languages. If a licensed lawyer is on the team, that is a separate, substantiable claim to add later — it is not this.
Substance — blocks Why YNWA, About and Proof
  1. Founding year and team size. Even “operating since 2019, six people” unlocks a whole section. Without it, that section does not exist.
  2. Any client willing to be named. One real testimonial with a name and a company outperforms twenty invented ones, and is the only kind we will publish. Logos alone, with permission, also work.
  3. Licences, registrations, memberships. Qatar Chamber membership, a CR number, any accreditation. These belong in the footer and in LocalBusiness schema.
  4. Which routes YNWA actually handles. Mainland/MOCI and QFC and free zones are listed on the current site. What about QSTP, Media City, and the Investment Promotion Agency route? We must not list a route the team does not work with.
Operations — blocks Contact and the assessment flow
  1. Where should enquiries land? Which inbox, is WhatsApp an official channel (it is how this market prefers to talk), and what is the expected response time we can honestly publish?
  2. Are there process facts we can publish? Typical formation timeline, standard document checklist, indicative fee ranges. Any one of these is worth more than a page of adjectives — and specificity is exactly where the better competitors are beating YNWA.
Arabic — blocks phase D
  1. Who signs off the Arabic? I will write professional Gulf-business Arabic rather than translate the English mechanically, but Arabic copy for a Qatari audience needs a native reviewer inside the business. Also: does the firm have an established Arabic name, or do we transliterate?
